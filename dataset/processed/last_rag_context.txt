Question: What are the differences between gross horsepower and net horsepower, and how do these measurements affect the way we understand an engine's power output?
Answer: Gross horsepower is a measurement of an engine's power output at the flywheel, without the engine installed in a vehicle, and without any accessories or parasitic losses. Net horsepower, also known as base horsepower, is a measurement of an engine's power output with the engine installed in a vehicle, and with all accessories and parasitic losses taken into account. The LT-1 engine's power output was originally rated at 370 gross horsepower, but was later rated at 255 net horsepower, reflecting the power lost to accessories and parasitic losses. This change in measurement methodology affects the way we understand an engine's power output, as it provides a more realistic representation of the engine's power output in a real-world driving scenario.
